The gap

When your tools don't talk to each other

Most businesses run on a patchwork: a CRM, spreadsheets, an email platform, a payment system and maybe an online store. When none of them are connected, the people in between become the integration.

  • Double entry. The same customer or job typed into two or three systems.
  • Missed handoffs. A sale closes but payroll, reporting or follow-up doesn't hear about it.
  • Numbers that don't match. Every system tells a slightly different story.
  • Rip-and-replace pressure. Software vendors pushing you to switch everything instead of connecting what works.

Connecting what you already have is almost always cheaper than replacing it.

Common questions

CRM integration FAQ

Do we have to switch CRMs?

No. We work with the tools you already have.

What if our software doesn't have an API?

We'll check on a call. Some platforms allow scheduled exports or webhooks instead; some genuinely can't be connected, and we'll tell you.

Where does the connected data live?

Typically in your own Google Workspace, so you own it.

What happens if a platform changes its API?

Integrations sometimes need updates when vendors change things. On a retainer, we monitor and fix them.

What does it cost?

A specific integration is a fixed-quote project. Ongoing monitoring can be part of a monthly retainer.
