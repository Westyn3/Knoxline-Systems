Imagine a homeowner checks out your company on Google.

Your Business Profile says you open at 7 AM. Your website says 8 AM.

Your profile lists you as "Smith Roofing." Your website footer says "Smith Roofing & Exteriors, Inc."

Your profile shows one phone number. Your website shows a different one.

None of that is a disaster on its own. But it plants a small question in the customer's mind: **which one is right?**

Google is asking the same question.

Your website and your Google Business Profile are two of the most important places your business lives online. When they match, they reinforce each other. When they don't, they create doubt.

The simplest way we can put it:

**A website that matches your Google Business Profile tells Google you are exactly who you say you are.**

In this post, we will walk through everything that should match, line by line, and show how we did it on our own website at Knoxline Systems in October 2026. We'll also share what happened in Google Search Console afterward, with the honest caveats.

## Why Consistency Matters to Google

Google's stated framework for local results comes down to three things: **relevance, distance and prominence.**

Relevance is how well your business matches what someone searched for. Distance is how far you are from the searcher or the area they searched. Prominence is how well known and trusted your business is.

Consistent business information supports all three.

When your website and your profile describe the same services, Google has a clearer picture of what you are relevant for. When they list the same service areas, Google has a clearer picture of where you work. When your name, phone and details line up across the web, it is easier for Google to connect everything that is said about you back to one real business.

Think of it like a background check.

If every document says the same thing, the story holds together. If your documents disagree, someone has to stop and figure out which one is true.

You want to make that job easy.

## Start With NAP: Name, Address, Phone

In local SEO, people talk about "NAP." It stands for **Name, Address and Phone number.** These are the basics, and they need to match exactly.

### Name

Use your real business name, exactly as it appears on your profile.

If your legal name is "Smith Roofing, LLC" and your profile says "Smith Roofing," that is fine. Many businesses show the brand name on the profile and include the legal name on the website. But be deliberate about it. The brand name should be identical everywhere customers see it, and the legal name should appear somewhere sensible on the site, like the footer, the about page or the structured data.

What you don't want is three slightly different names floating around: "Smith Roofing," "Smith Roofing Co." and "Smith Roofing and Gutters."

And never stuff keywords into your profile name. Google's guidelines say the name should match your real-world name. We covered this in more detail in [our post on denied Business Profiles](/blog/google-business-profile-denied-service-area-business/).

### Address

If you are a storefront business, your address should be written the same way on your website and your profile.

If you are a service-area business with a hidden address, don't contradict that on your website by featuring your home address as if it were a storefront. Instead, focus your website on the areas you serve.

### Phone number

Use one main phone number, and make it the same everywhere. On your profile, in your website header, in your footer, on your contact page and in your structured data.

If you use a tracking number for ads, think carefully about where it shows. Your main business number should still be the consistent one across your core listings.

## Beyond NAP: What Else Should Match

Name, address and phone are just the start. Here is the rest of the list.

### Hours

Your hours should be identical on your website and your profile.

That includes special hours. If you close for Thanksgiving or Christmas, update the profile, and if your site shows hours, update it too.

### Services

The services you list on your profile should have a real home on your website. If you list "septic pumping" as a service on your profile, there should be a page or section on your site that clearly explains your septic pumping service.

### Categories

Your profile categories tell Google what type of business you are. Your website should clearly support those categories.

If your primary category is "Roofing contractor," the website should make it obvious that roofing is what you do. If you have secondary categories like "Gutter cleaning service," the website should have content that supports that work too.

### Service areas

Your service areas on the profile should match the areas you talk about on the website. Same towns, same counties, same list.

If your profile says you serve 20 areas and your website only mentions three, the two are telling different stories.

### Branding and photos

This one is less technical, but it still matters. Your logo, your truck, your crew and your job photos should look like the same company on both.

## Structured Data: Saying It in Google's Language

Matching the visible text on your website is step one. Step two is adding **structured data**, also called schema markup.

Structured data is code on your pages that describes your business in a standard format search engines can read. It doesn't change what visitors see. It gives Google a clean, labeled version of your information.

On our own site, we added:

- **Business schema (ProfessionalService)** with our legal name, hours, service areas, logo, founder and founding date.
- **sameAs links,** including a permanent Google Maps link to our Business Profile.
- **WebSite schema** for the site as a whole.
- **Breadcrumbs** on pages and posts.
- **Article schema** on blog posts.
- **FAQ schema** on service pages.

### Why the sameAs link matters

The sameAs property is a way of saying, "This website and this other profile are the same business."

By including a permanent Google Maps link to our Business Profile in sameAs, we are pointing directly from our website to our profile. It is one more way to tie the two together clearly.

### We tested everything

We ran our pages through **Google's Rich Results Test** and the **Schema.org validator.** Both returned **zero errors.**

That matters. Structured data with mistakes in it is not helping anyone. Test it every time you change it.

## What Happened After the Changes

Here is what we saw in Google Search Console after the changes. We want to be careful here: we made several improvements around the same time, including speed fixes and title changes, so we can't say which single change did what. This is what the combined work looked like.

- **Impressions** went from about 6 per day to about 29 per day in the first week.
- **"knoxville google ads"** moved from an average position of 54 (on a three-month view) to about 20 within roughly three weeks.
- **"website design for contractor knoxville"** moved from about 43 to about 10–11.
- **"septic company local seo"** reached about position 8.

And here is the honest part: **clicks were still near zero** at that point.

That is normal. Rankings tend to move first. Clicks follow when you reach page one for searches people actually use. Organic SEO usually takes months, and around six months is a fair expectation before judging results.

We share this not as a promise of what will happen for your business, but as a real, transparent look at what consistency and clean technical work looked like on our own site.

## How to Audit Your Own Website and Profile

You can do a basic version of this yourself in about 30 minutes.

Open your Google Business Profile in one window and your website in another. Then go down this list:

1. **Business name.** Is it identical? Is your legal name shown somewhere sensible on the site?
2. **Phone number.** Same number in the profile, header, footer and contact page?
3. **Address.** Written the same way, or hidden on the profile and not featured as a storefront on the site if you are a service-area business?
4. **Hours.** Exactly the same, including holiday hours?
5. **Primary category.** Does your website clearly support it?
6. **Secondary categories.** Is there a page or section that backs up each one?
7. **Services.** Does every service on your profile have a real home on your website?
8. **Service areas.** Is it the same list in both places?
9. **Website link.** Does your profile link to the right page on your site?
10. **Structured data.** Does your site have business schema with the same information? Does it pass Google's Rich Results Test?

Write down every mismatch. Then fix them, one by one.

### Which one do you fix?

Start by deciding what is actually true about your business today. Then update whichever one is wrong.

Sometimes the profile is out of date. Sometimes the website is. Sometimes both are a little off. The goal is not to make one copy the other. It is to make both accurately reflect the real business.

## Don't Forget the Rest of the Web

Your website and your profile are the most important pair. But your business information also lives on other sites: Facebook, industry directories, your local chamber of commerce, the Better Business Bureau, review sites and more.

Once your website and profile match, check those too. The goal is the same: one business, one name, one phone number, one set of hours, everywhere.

You don't need to chase every directory on the internet. Start with the ones customers actually use in your trade and your area.

## Common Mismatches We See

A few examples from the kinds of things that come up when we review home-service businesses:

- The profile says "open 24 hours" but the website lists regular hours.
- The website has an old phone number from before the business switched carriers.
- The profile lists 15 service areas but the website only mentions the home city.
- The profile's primary category is something the website barely mentions.
- The business name has a keyword added on the profile but not anywhere else.
- The website was built by one company, the profile set up by another, and nobody compared them.

That last one is the root of most of these problems. **No one is responsible for keeping them in sync.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does my website have to match my Google Business Profile exactly?

The core details should: business name, phone number, address (if shown), hours, services and service areas. Small formatting differences are less of a concern than real contradictions, but exact matches leave no room for confusion.

### What is NAP consistency?

NAP stands for Name, Address and Phone number. NAP consistency means those details are the same on your website, your Google Business Profile and other places your business is listed online.

### Should my legal name or my brand name be on the profile?

Your profile name should be the real-world name customers know you by, as Google's guidelines describe. If your legal name is different, like an LLC name, you can show it on your website and in your structured data so the two are clearly connected.

### What is a sameAs link?

It is a property in structured data that tells search engines other web pages, like your Google Business Profile or social profiles, represent the same business as your website. We include a permanent Google Maps link to our own profile in ours.

### Will matching my website and profile get me to the top of Google?

No one can promise that. Consistency is part of a strong foundation, along with a useful website, a complete profile and a steady stream of real reviews. It makes it easier for Google to understand and trust your business, but rankings depend on many factors.
