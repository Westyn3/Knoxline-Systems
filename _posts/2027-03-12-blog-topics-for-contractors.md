Most contractor blogs die the same way.

Someone says, "We should have a blog."

Three posts go up. One about the company's history. One called "Welcome to Our New Website." One about a holiday.

Then nothing for two years.

That's not a writing problem. It's a topic problem and a process problem.

The posts were about the company, not about the questions homeowners are typing into Google. And there was no system to keep them coming.

A blog that brings calls looks different. It answers real questions, in plain language, on a steady schedule. A homeowner searching "how often should a septic tank be pumped" finds your answer, reads it, sees that you serve their area and calls.

That's the goal.

In this post, we'll walk through the blog topics that tend to match how homeowners search, how to come up with your own list, and how automatic weekly publishing keeps the whole thing running without you having to remember.

## Why a Contractor Blog Is Worth It

Your service pages do the heavy lifting. A roof replacement page, an AC repair page, a septic pumping page. Those are the pages built to rank for "service + area" searches and convert visitors into calls.

But homeowners search for a lot more than services.

They search for problems:

- "Why is my AC blowing warm air?"
- "What does a soggy yard over a septic drain field mean?"
- "How do I know if my roof needs to be replaced?"

They search for costs:

- "How much does a new septic system cost?"
- "Roof repair vs. replacement cost"

They search for timing:

- "When should I service my furnace?"
- "Best time of year to replace a roof"

Your service pages can't answer every one of those questions without becoming bloated. Blog posts can.

Each good post is another door into your website. Someone walks in with a question, gets a straight answer, and sees a company that clearly knows the work.

**A blog post answers the question. Your service page gets the call.**

## Topic Type 1: Question-Based Posts

This is the foundation. Take the questions your customers ask you on the phone, at the door and in estimates, and answer each one in a post.

You already know these questions. You've answered them hundreds of times.

### Examples by trade

**Septic:**

- How often should a septic tank be pumped?
- What should I not put down the drain with a septic system?
- What are the signs of a failing drain field?
- Do I need a septic inspection when buying a house?

**Roofing:**

- How long does a shingle roof last?
- Can you put a new roof over an old one?
- What should I do right after a roof leak?
- How do I know if I have storm damage?

**HVAC:**

- Why is my AC running but not cooling?
- How often should I change my air filter?
- Is it worth repairing an older system or replacing it?
- What size system does my house need?

**Plumbing:**

- Why does my water heater make popping noises?
- What causes low water pressure in the whole house?
- What should I do if a pipe bursts?

### How to write them

- **Put the question in the title.** That's often how people search.
- **Answer it early.** Don't make readers scroll through three paragraphs of background.
- **Then explain.** Causes, options, what a homeowner can safely check, and when to call a pro.
- **Be honest about safety.** If something is dangerous to DIY, say so plainly.
- **Link to the matching service page.** A post about failing drain fields should link to your septic repair page.

## Topic Type 2: Cost Guides

Homeowners want to know what something will cost before they call. Many contractors avoid this topic because every job is different.

That's exactly why a cost guide helps.

You don't have to publish a price list. You can explain **what drives the cost**:

- For a septic system: soil conditions, system type, tank size, site access, permits and grading.
- For a roof: roof size, pitch, materials, layers to remove, decking repairs.
- For HVAC: system type, efficiency level, ductwork condition, home size.

A good cost guide helps the homeowner understand why quotes vary. It also filters your leads. Someone who reads your cost guide and still calls is a more informed customer.

If you do share price ranges, use your own real numbers and keep them current. Never make up figures.

### Example titles

- "What Affects the Cost of a New Septic System?"
- "Roof Repair or Roof Replacement: How to Think About the Cost"
- "Why Two HVAC Quotes Can Be So Different"

## Topic Type 3: Local Project Stories

This is the content your competitors can't copy.

A post about a real job in a real town shows Google and homeowners that you actually work there. It also shows your work in a way a generic service page can't.

A good project post includes:

- **The town or area**, stated naturally.
- **The problem** the homeowner had.
- **What you found** when you got there.
- **What you did** and why.
- **Photos**, before and after if you have them.
- **What the homeowner should know** if they face something similar.

A few cautions:

- **Get the customer's permission** before sharing details or photos of their property.
- **Don't publish exact addresses.**
- **Keep it true.** Project stories only work if they're real.

Project posts also strengthen your service area pages. A post about a drain field repair in one town can link to your page for that area, and that page can link back. We covered how to build area pages the right way in [Service Area Pages That Rank](/blog/service-area-pages-that-rank/).

## Topic Type 4: Seasonal Maintenance

Home-service work follows the seasons, and so do searches.

Planning posts around the calendar lets you publish what people are about to search for:

- **Late winter and early spring:** spring roof checks after winter weather, AC tune-ups before the heat, septic pumping before the busy season, grading and drainage before spring rains.
- **Summer:** AC troubleshooting, storm damage, irrigation and water use with a septic system.
- **Fall:** furnace maintenance, gutter cleaning, preparing pipes for freezing temperatures.
- **Winter:** frozen pipes, heating problems, ice dams on roofs.

Seasonal posts can be updated and republished each year. A good "spring roof checklist" post can earn its keep for years with small updates.

## Topic Type 5: "Should I Repair or Replace?"

These decision posts tend to attract homeowners who are close to spending money.

- "Repair or Replace Your Water Heater?"
- "When Does a Septic System Need Replacement Instead of Repair?"
- "Should You Repair a Leaking Roof or Replace It?"

Lay out the factors honestly. Age, condition, cost of repeated repairs, safety. Then explain how you help homeowners decide. A balanced post builds more trust than a post that always says "replace."

## Topic Type 6: Explaining Your Process

Homeowners are nervous about what they don't understand.

Posts that explain what happens during a job can calm that nervousness before the first call:

- "What Happens During a Septic Inspection"
- "What to Expect on Roof Replacement Day"
- "What a Furnace Tune-Up Actually Includes"

These posts are easy to write because you know your process. They also make your sales conversations easier because customers arrive already knowing the steps.

## Topics to Skip

Not every post is worth writing.

- **Company announcements nobody searches for.** "We got a new truck" is fine for Facebook. It rarely brings search traffic.
- **Generic posts copied from somewhere else.** They add nothing and can hurt trust.
- **Keyword-stuffed posts.** A post with "best roofer near me" crammed into every sentence reads badly and doesn't help.
- **Topics outside your work.** A plumber doesn't need a post on interior design trends.

## How to Build Your Own Topic List in 30 Minutes

You don't need fancy research tools to start.

1. **Ask your office staff** what questions customers ask on the phone. Write them all down.
2. **Ask your techs or crew leads** what homeowners ask on site.
3. **Look at your estimates.** What do people need explained before they say yes?
4. **Type your main services into Google** and look at the "People also ask" questions and the suggested searches.
5. **Check Google Search Console.** Look at the queries your site already appears for. Questions you're showing up for, but don't have a dedicated answer to, are great post ideas.
6. **Map topics to the calendar.** Put seasonal topics a few weeks before the season starts.

That's easily a year of posts.

## Why Consistency Matters More Than Perfection

A single great post is helpful. A steady flow of useful posts builds something larger.

Here's the mechanism, in plain terms:

- **New pages give Google new reasons to visit your site.** When new content shows up regularly, and your sitemap and feed list it, Google has a reason to keep crawling.
- **Each post can target a different question.** Over time, your site covers more of what homeowners search for.
- **Posts link to service pages.** That internal linking helps Google understand which pages matter most.
- **Your site doesn't go stale.** A site that was last updated years ago tells customers something, too.

None of this produces overnight results. Organic SEO usually takes months, and around six months is a fair expectation before you see a real picture. That's exactly why consistency matters more than any single post.

## A Simple Post Template

If you write your own posts, here's a structure that works for most question-based topics:

1. **Hook:** describe the problem the way the homeowner experiences it.
2. **Short answer:** answer the question in two or three sentences.
3. **The details:** causes, options and what affects the answer.
4. **What a homeowner can safely check.**
5. **When to call a professional.**
6. **A short FAQ** with related questions.
7. **A call to action** linking to the matching service page and your contact page.

Keep paragraphs short. Most readers will be on a phone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should a contractor blog?

Weekly is a strong, sustainable rhythm if you have a system. Consistency matters more than volume. One useful post a week, every week, beats ten posts in January and nothing after.

### How long should a contractor blog post be?

Long enough to fully answer the question. Some topics need a few hundred words. Detailed guides may need a couple thousand. Don't pad posts to hit a number.

### Will blogging get me to the top of Google?

No one can promise that. A blog gives you more chances to show up for the questions homeowners ask, and it supports your service pages. Results build over months.

### Can I write about my competitors' service areas?

Only write about areas you actually serve. Your content should match your real service area and your Google Business Profile.

### Do I need photos in every post?

Not always, but real photos from your own jobs help, especially in project stories. Compress them so they don't slow the page down.
