Merry Christmas from Knoxline Systems.

If you're reading this today, you're either taking a quiet minute with your coffee or you're on call. Either way, thank you. Somebody's furnace, septic system or water heater is going to need help this week, and home-service people are the ones who show up.

For a lot of contractors, though, the stretch between Christmas and the first week of January is slower than usual. The phone rings less. The schedule has gaps.

That makes it a good time to do something most businesses never get around to:

**Check whether your website and Google Business Profile still tell the truth about your business.**

Not a redesign. Not a big marketing project. Just a checkup.

This is the checklist we use. Most of it takes a few minutes per item. You can do the whole thing in an afternoon, or split it across a few slow mornings.

## Part 1: Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing a homeowner sees. Many people call straight from it without ever visiting your website. Start here.

### 1. Set Your Holiday Hours

This is the most time-sensitive item on the list.

Google Business Profile lets you set special hours for specific dates, separate from your regular weekly hours. Use it for:

- Christmas Eve and Christmas Day
- New Year's Eve and New Year's Day
- Any days your office is closed but emergency service is available

If you offer 24/7 emergency service over the holidays, make that clear. If you're closed, say so. A homeowner who drives to your shop or calls during "open" hours and gets nothing is not going to be a fan.

Google may also prompt you to confirm holiday hours. Don't ignore those prompts.

### 2. Confirm Your Regular Hours, Phone and Name

While you're in the profile, check the basics:

- **Business name:** Is it your real business name, exactly as you use it? Google requires the name to reflect the real-world business. Adding keywords like "Best Roofer Knoxville" to the name is against Google's guidelines and can cause problems.
- **Phone number:** Does it ring where you want it to ring? Did you change lines this year?
- **Regular hours:** Did anything change? New Saturday hours? Shorter Fridays?
- **Website link:** Does it go to the right page and load without errors?

### 3. Review Your Categories and Services

Did you add a service this year? Drop one?

Your primary category and additional categories tell Google what kind of business you are. Your services list adds detail. If you started doing drain field repairs, mini-split installs or metal roofing this year, make sure the profile reflects it.

Only list what you actually do.

### 4. Check Your Service Areas

If you're a service-area business (you go to the customer rather than the customer coming to you), Google lets you hide your street address and list the areas you serve instead.

Look at the list. Does it still match where you actually work? Did you start taking jobs in a new county? Stop going to one that was too far?

### 5. Add New Photos

Most contractors finish dozens or hundreds of jobs a year and add almost none of them to their profile.

Go through your phone. Pick a handful of good photos from this year:

- Finished projects
- Your trucks and equipment with your branding
- Your crew on the job (with their permission)
- Before-and-after shots

Real photos of real work help a homeowner trust that you're an actual, active company. Skip stock photos.

### 6. Reply to Every Unanswered Review

Scroll through your reviews from this year. Any without a reply?

Write a short, genuine response to each one. Thank happy customers. For unhappy ones, stay calm, keep it brief and offer a way to talk offline.

If you don't have a consistent process for getting new reviews, that's worth fixing in January. We laid out a full approach in [a Google review system that runs itself](/blog/google-review-system-for-home-service-businesses/).

## Part 2: Your Website

Now open your own website. Do this on your phone first, since that's how most homeowners will see it, then on a computer.

### 7. Read Your Homepage Like a Stranger

Pretend you've never heard of your company. Within a few seconds, can you tell:

- What the company does?
- Where it works?
- How to call?

If any of those take digging, write it down. That's a January fix.

### 8. Update the Obvious Stuff

Small things that make a site look abandoned:

- **Copyright year in the footer.** If it says 2021, a customer notices.
- **Old promotions.** A "Summer AC Tune-Up Special" banner in December looks neglected.
- **Staff who left.** Remove names and photos of people no longer with you.
- **Services you dropped.** Don't advertise work you no longer do.
- **Old phone numbers or addresses.** Check the header, footer, contact page and any embedded maps.

### 9. Check That Your Website Matches Your Google Business Profile

This is one we care about a lot.

Your business name, phone number, hours and service areas should be the same on your website as on your Google Business Profile. When they disagree, you're sending Google and customers mixed signals.

When we updated our own site in October, we matched it to our profile exactly: the legal name "Knoxline Systems, LLC," the same phone, the same Mon–Fri 7 AM–6 PM hours, and the same 20 service areas listed on the profile. We wrote up the whole process in [why your website should match your Google Business Profile](/blog/match-website-to-google-business-profile/).

If you set holiday hours on Google, consider posting them on your website too.

### 10. Click Every Link in Your Menu

Broken links happen. Pages get renamed, services get removed, and old links keep pointing at nothing.

Click through every item in your navigation menu and footer. Click the buttons on your homepage. Anything that lands on an error page needs fixing or removing.

If you have a bigger site, Google Search Console (more on that below) can show pages Google tried to reach and couldn't.

### 11. Test Every Contact Form

This one is important and almost nobody does it.

Fill out your own contact form. Submit it. Then check:

- Did the confirmation message show up?
- Did the email arrive in the right inbox?
- Did it land in spam?
- Did the information come through complete?

Forms can quietly fail for months when an email address changes or a plugin breaks. Every submission lost that way is a potential customer who thought they reached you.

Test every form on the site, not just the main one.

### 12. Tap Your Phone Number

On your phone, tap the phone number on your website. Does it start a call? Is it the right number?

A phone number that isn't tappable on mobile is a small annoyance that costs real calls. It should be a single tap, everywhere it appears.

### 13. Check Your Speed

Go to Google PageSpeed Insights, enter your homepage address and run the test. Look at the mobile results.

You don't need to chase a perfect score. You're looking for big problems: very slow loading, huge images, or warnings that keep coming up.

When we tested our own site, mobile performance came in at 76. The biggest issue was about 2.1 seconds of render-blocking requests, mostly from Google Fonts. We self-hosted the fonts with preload, loaded analytics after the page, and made the main headline paint immediately.

The point isn't our number. It's that even a carefully built site can have one specific problem slowing it down, and the test usually tells you what it is.

### 14. Look at Your Site on a Phone, Sideways and Up Close

Scroll every main page on your phone. Watch for:

- Text too small to read without zooming
- Buttons too close together to tap
- Pages that scroll sideways
- Pop-ups that cover the content

These are the kinds of problems that make a homeowner hit the back button and call the next company.

## Part 3: Google Search Console

If your website is connected to Google Search Console, set aside 20 minutes for this part. If it isn't, setting it up is a great January project.

Search Console is free. It shows you how your site appears in Google search: what people searched, how often you showed up, where you ranked on average and how many clicked.

### 15. Look at Your Performance Report

Open the Performance report and set the date range to the last 12 months, or the longest range available. Look at:

- **Impressions:** how often your site appeared in search results.
- **Clicks:** how often people clicked through.
- **Average position:** roughly where you showed up.
- **Queries:** the actual words people typed.

Are the queries the ones you'd expect? Are you showing up for your main services and towns?

Rankings often move before clicks do. When we changed our own site this fall, impressions went from about 6 per day to about 29 per day in the first week. "website design for contractor knoxville" moved from an average position of about 43 to about 10–11. But clicks were still near zero at that point. Clicks usually pick up once you reach page one. Knowing that keeps you from panicking or giving up too early.

### 16. Check Indexing

Look at the pages report to see which of your pages Google has indexed and which it hasn't. If your most important service pages aren't indexed, that's a problem worth solving.

### 17. Confirm Your Sitemap

Make sure your sitemap is submitted and Google can read it. A sitemap helps Google find every page on your site, including new ones.

## Part 4: Plan for Spring

For most home-service companies, spring is busy. Roofers deal with storm season. HVAC companies switch from heating to cooling. Septic and grading work picks up once the ground dries out.

Organic SEO usually takes months to show results. Six months is a fair expectation. That means work you start in January can be paying off when the busy season actually arrives.

### 18. Write Down the Services You Want More Of

Not every job is equally valuable. Which services would you most like the phone to ring for in 2027?

Write down your top three. Those are the services that deserve their own strong page on your website.

### 19. Write Down the Areas You Want to Grow Into

Same question for geography. Where do you want more work? Those towns and counties might need clear coverage on your website and in your service area list.

### 20. Decide What You'll Change and Who's Doing It

Take everything from this checklist that needs fixing and put it in one list. Then split it:

- Things you can fix yourself this week
- Things that need your web person
- Things that need a bigger decision (a new site, a new service page, a review system)

Put dates next to them. A list with no dates tends to look the same next December.

## The Checklist, Short Version

Print this or save it:

**Google Business Profile**
- Holiday hours set
- Name, phone, regular hours, website link confirmed
- Categories and services current
- Service areas current
- New photos added
- All reviews replied to

**Website**
- Homepage passes the stranger test
- Footer year, old promos, old staff, dropped services cleaned up
- Name, phone, hours and areas match the Google profile
- All menu and button links work
- Every form submitted and received
- Phone number tappable on mobile
- PageSpeed checked on mobile
- Every main page checked on a phone

**Search Console**
- Performance report reviewed
- Indexing checked
- Sitemap confirmed

**Spring plan**
- Top three services chosen
- Growth areas chosen
- Fix list with owners and dates

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How often should a home-service business update its website?

The key information (services, areas, phone, hours) should be updated whenever it changes. Beyond that, a full checkup like this once or twice a year catches most problems. Regular new content, like a blog, gives Google more reasons to keep crawling your site.

### Do holiday hours on Google Business Profile affect rankings?

We'd focus on the customer side. Accurate holiday hours keep people from calling or showing up when you're closed, and they show the profile is actively managed. That's reason enough to set them.

### What if my website fails half of this checklist?

That's common, and it's fixable. Start with the items that cost you calls directly: broken forms, a phone number that isn't tappable, wrong hours. Then work on the bigger issues. If the site is old enough that fixing it costs more than rebuilding it, that's a decision worth making early in the year.

### Is Google Search Console worth setting up for a small contractor?

Yes. It's free, it comes straight from Google, and it's the most direct way to see what searches your site actually appears for. Even a small site benefits from knowing that.

## Start the Year With a Site That Works

Your website doesn't need to be fancy. It needs to be accurate, fast, easy to use on a phone, and consistent with your Google Business Profile.

Most of that comes down to checking things regularly instead of building a site once and walking away.
