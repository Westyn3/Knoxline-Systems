Every home-service owner eventually asks some version of this question:

**"Should I be paying for Google Ads, or should I be doing SEO?"**

It's a fair question. Both put your business in front of people searching Google. Both cost money. Both get sold hard by marketing companies.

But they work in very different ways.

Google Ads is like renting a billboard on the busiest road in town. You pay, it shows up. Stop paying, it disappears.

SEO is more like building a storefront on that road. It takes longer to build. But once it's up, it keeps standing.

Neither one is "better" in every situation. A roofing company after a hailstorm, a septic company trying to fill a slow month and an HVAC company opening a new service area may all need different answers.

This post lays out how each one works, when we'd recommend each, and why for most home-service companies the real answer starts with something underneath both.

## How Google Ads Works, in Plain English

Google Ads lets you pay to appear in Google search results for searches you choose.

Here's the basic idea:

- **You choose keywords**, like "septic pumping" or "AC repair."
- **You set a budget** and how much you're willing to pay.
- **Google runs an auction** each time someone searches. Your bid and the quality of your ad and landing page both factor in.
- **You pay when someone clicks.** That's why it's called pay-per-click.

You can target specific locations, so a Knoxville HVAC company can show ads to people searching in Knoxville, Farragut, Maryville or wherever it actually works. You can set the hours ads run. You can choose which page someone lands on.

### What Google Ads does well

- **Speed.** Ads can start showing soon after a campaign is approved and live. You don't wait months.
- **Control.** You choose services, areas, budgets and schedules.
- **Testing.** You can learn quickly which services and messages get calls.
- **Filling gaps.** Slow season, new service, new area, new business. Ads can help bridge the gap while organic visibility builds.

### What Google Ads costs you

- **Every click costs money**, whether it turns into a job or not.
- **When you stop paying, the visibility stops.** There's no carryover.
- **Competition sets the price.** In competitive trades and areas, clicks can get expensive.
- **Bad setup wastes money quietly.** Wrong keywords, wrong locations or bad tracking can burn budget for months before anyone notices.

That last point deserves its own section.

## The Ghost Conversion Problem

When we audited the Google Ads account of a Georgia home-service company, we found that **nearly 40% of the reported "conversions" were map taps and direction clicks, not real leads.**

On the dashboard, the account looked like it was producing far more leads than it was.

The owner was making budget decisions based on numbers that included people tapping a map pin.

It's an easy mistake to make. Google Ads can count many different actions as conversions, depending on how the account is set up. If no one checks what's being counted, the reports can look much better than reality.

We wrote up the full story in [Are Your Google Ads Leads Real? How We Found 40% Ghost Conversions](/blog/are-your-google-ads-leads-real-how-we-found-40-ghost-conversions-in-a-home-service-ad-account/).

The lesson for this comparison:

**Google Ads is only as good as its tracking.** Before you judge whether ads "work," make sure the account is counting real calls and real form submissions, not taps and clicks that never become customers.

## How SEO Works, in Plain English

SEO, or search engine optimization, is the work of earning free visibility in Google's results.

For a home-service business, that really means two places:

1. **The regular search results**, where your website pages show up.
2. **The map results**, where your Google Business Profile shows up.

Google has said local results are based mainly on **relevance, distance and prominence**. In plain terms: does your business match what was searched, how close are you to the searcher, and how well-known and trusted is your business?

You don't pay per click for any of it.

You earn it by building:

- **A fast, clear website** with a page for each service and honest service area content.
- **A complete, accurate Google Business Profile** that matches your website.
- **A steady flow of legitimate reviews.**
- **Structured data** that tells Google exactly who you are.
- **Useful content**, like blog posts answering real customer questions.

### What SEO does well

- **It compounds.** Every page, review and improvement keeps working after it's done.
- **No cost per click.** Organic calls don't come with a per-click bill.
- **Trust.** Many homeowners trust a strong map listing with real reviews.
- **It strengthens everything else**, including your ads, because better pages often help ad performance too.

### What SEO costs you

- **Time.** Organic SEO usually takes months. Around six months is a fair expectation before you see a real picture.
- **Patience.** Rankings move first. Clicks and calls tend to follow once you reach the first page.
- **No guarantees.** No honest company can promise a specific ranking.

## Local Services Ads: A Third Option to Know About

In many home-service categories, Google also offers **Local Services Ads**. These can show at the very top of some searches, often with a badge showing the business has gone through Google's screening.

A few general points:

- **They're typically charged per lead**, not per click.
- **Businesses go through a screening and verification process** before ads can run.
- **Availability depends on your category and location.** Not every trade or area has them.
- **Your reviews and profile matter** to how they show.

The details of Local Services Ads change over time, so check Google's current requirements for your trade before planning around them. For some home-service businesses, they can be a useful addition alongside regular Google Ads and SEO.

## Foundation First

Here's the part most ad sellers skip.

Both Google Ads and SEO send people to the same places: **your website and your Google Business Profile.**

If those are weak, every channel suffers.

- **Ads** send paid clicks to a slow page with a hidden phone number. You pay for the click either way.
- **SEO** has nothing solid to rank. Thin pages and a mismatched profile give Google little reason to trust you.

That's why we think about it in order:

1. **Fix the foundation.** Fast, mobile-friendly website. Clear service pages. Honest service areas. GBP that matches the site. Real tracking.
2. **Start SEO**, because it takes the longest to build.
3. **Add Google Ads where speed matters**, with clean conversion tracking from day one.
4. **Measure leads, not just clicks or rankings.**

A homeowner whose AC dies in July is going to judge your company in seconds. Whether they came from an ad or the map, your site and profile have to close the deal.

## When to Lean on Google Ads

Ads make the most sense when:

- **You're new** and need calls before organic visibility builds.
- **You're launching a new service** and want to test demand.
- **You're expanding into a new area** where you don't show up organically yet.
- **You have capacity to fill**, like a slow month with crews standing around.
- **The search is urgent and valuable**, like emergency repairs, and you want to be at the top right now.

Ads are a faucet. You can turn it up or down. That flexibility is the main value.

## When to Lean on SEO

SEO makes the most sense when:

- **You plan to be in business for years** and want visibility that builds.
- **Your market is reachable**, meaning local competitors haven't fully built out their sites and profiles.
- **You want to lower your dependence on paid clicks** over time.
- **You have a reputation worth showing**, and a review system can capture it.

SEO is an asset. It takes time to build, but it keeps working.

## When to Use Both

For many established home-service companies, the answer is both, working together.

- **SEO covers the core services and areas** where you already show up well.
- **Ads cover the gaps**: services or areas where you don't rank yet, peak seasons and urgent searches.
- **As organic visibility grows**, you can shift ad budget toward the places SEO doesn't reach.
- **Search Console and ad reports together** show where you're visible for free and where you're paying.

The worst setup is paying for ads on searches you already win organically, while nobody works on the searches you don't.

## What Good Google Ads Management Looks Like

If you hire someone for Google Ads management for a home-services business, here's what we'd expect them to handle:

- **Conversion tracking audited first.** Only real calls and form submissions counted as leads.
- **Location targeting that matches your real service area.**
- **Keywords tied to services you actually want**, with irrelevant searches excluded.
- **Ad schedules that match when you can answer the phone.**
- **Landing pages that match the ad.** An ad for septic pumping should land on a septic pumping page, not the homepage.
- **Plain-English reporting** on cost per real lead, not just clicks and impressions.

If your current reports can't tell you how many real phone calls your ads produced last month, that's the first thing to fix.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Google Ads or SEO better for a new home-service business?

Usually both, in different roles. Ads can generate calls while the website, Google Business Profile and reviews build organic visibility. Start the foundation and SEO early, because they take the longest.

### How long does SEO take for a contractor?

Organic SEO usually takes months. Around six months is a fair expectation to see a real picture. Rankings tend to move before clicks and calls show up.

### Do Google Ads help my organic rankings?

Paying for ads doesn't directly raise your organic rankings. But the work that makes ads perform better, like clear landing pages and a fast site, also supports SEO.

### What are ghost conversions in Google Ads?

They're actions counted as conversions that aren't real leads, like map taps or direction clicks. In one Georgia home-service account we audited, they made up nearly 40% of reported conversions.

### Can I stop running ads once SEO is working?

Some businesses reduce ads as organic visibility grows. Others keep ads for specific services, areas or seasons. Use your own lead data to decide.

## The Bottom Line

Google Ads buys visibility now. SEO builds visibility that lasts.

Both depend on the same foundation: a website and Google Business Profile that make it easy for a homeowner to trust you and call.

Get the foundation right. Start the long-term work early. Use ads where speed matters, with tracking you can trust.
