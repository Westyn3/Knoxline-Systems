## First, What Is a Service-Area Business?

Google separates businesses into two broad types.

**Storefront businesses** have a location customers visit. A restaurant. A retail store. A showroom.

**Service-area businesses** go to the customer. A roofer. A septic pumper. An HVAC technician. A mobile appliance repair tech. A handyman.

Many home-service companies are service-area businesses. You may have a shop, an office or a yard, but customers are not walking in to buy something. You go to them.

That difference matters a lot for your profile.

Google's guidelines say service-area businesses that do not serve customers at their address should **hide that address** and instead list the areas they serve. Your address is still used for verification behind the scenes, but it does not show publicly.

If you do serve customers at your location during your listed hours, you can show the address as well. If you don't, you shouldn't.

Getting this wrong is one of the most common sources of trouble.

## Who Google Allows to Have a Business Profile

This is the part a lot of owners never read.

Google only allows Business Profiles for businesses that **make in-person contact with customers** during their stated hours. Either the customer comes to you, or you go to the customer.

That rules out some businesses entirely. An online-only business, for example, is generally not eligible.

For a home-service company, the in-person part is usually obvious. You are literally standing in the customer's yard or attic. But your profile and your website need to make that obvious to Google, too.

## Common Reasons a Service-Area Business Gets Denied

Google does not always tell you exactly why a profile failed. But when we look at denied or suspended profiles, the problems usually fall into a handful of categories.

### 1. The business name does not match the real business name

This is the big one.

Google's guidelines say your profile name should reflect your real-world business name, the one you actually use consistently on your signage, vehicles, paperwork and website.

What it should **not** include is extra keywords or locations stuffed in to help ranking.

If your company is "Smith Plumbing," your profile name is "Smith Plumbing." Not "Smith Plumbing - Best Emergency Plumber Knoxville TN 24/7."

Keyword stuffing the name is against Google's guidelines and can get a profile suspended. It also makes verification harder, because your name on Google no longer matches your name on your truck, your license or your website.

If your legal name and your brand name are different, that is normal. Many businesses operate under a trade name. The key is that the name on the profile is the name customers actually see when they deal with you, and that your documents can tie the two together.

### 2. Address problems

Address issues show up in a few ways:

- **Using an address you can't prove.** If you list an address, you need to be able to show it is connected to your business.
- **Using a P.O. box or a mailbox service.** Google's guidelines do not accept P.O. boxes or remote mailboxes as business locations.
- **Showing a home address as a storefront** when customers never come there. For a service-area business, hide the address.
- **Duplicate profiles.** Creating a second profile for the same business, or one at an address you don't really operate from, can cause problems for both.

### 3. Nothing shows in-person customer contact

If your profile, your website and your verification evidence don't make it clear that you serve customers in person, Google may decide the business is not eligible.

For a service-area business, that means being clear about the work you do at customer locations and the areas you cover.

### 4. No website, or a website that doesn't match

Google does not strictly require a website for a Business Profile.

But in our experience, a live website that matches your profile makes your business much easier to verify and much easier for Google to trust. When the name, phone number, hours, services and service areas on your website line up with your profile, it all supports the same story: this is a real business that does what it says.

When there is no website, or the website shows a different name, a different phone number or a different list of services, there is less to support your case.

### 5. Failed video verification

For many businesses, Google now asks for video verification. You record a short video showing your business, and Google reviews it.

Video verification fails more often than people expect, usually for practical reasons:

- The video does not clearly show a real business.
- There is nothing tying the business to the name on the profile.
- The video was edited, cut or stopped and restarted.
- The footage was too dark, too shaky or too quick to see anything.
- The person recording could not show they actually manage the business.

We will cover how to prepare for this below, because it is often the fastest fix.

### 6. Edits during review

Changing your name, category or address while a profile is being reviewed can trigger another review or reset the process. It is usually best to make your fixes, then leave the profile alone while Google looks at it.

## Before You Try Again: What to Prepare

Here is the part that actually gets profiles verified.

Before you request another review or try verification again, gather your proof. Think of it like preparing for an inspection. The more clearly you can show the business is real and that you manage it, the better.

### Branded vehicles and equipment

For a service-area business, this is often your strongest evidence.

- A work truck or van with your business name on it.
- Branded equipment, trailers or tools.
- Uniforms or shirts with your logo.

If your business name is on your truck and your truck is in the video, Google can see a real business that goes to customers. That fits exactly what a service-area profile is supposed to be.

If you don't have any branding on your vehicles or equipment yet, it is worth considering. Even a professionally made vehicle magnet or decal can help show the business is real.

### Business documents

Have these ready:

- **Your business license** from your city or county, if your area issues one.
- **Your LLC or corporation documents**, such as articles of organization filed with your state.
- **Trade licenses** if your work requires them, like a contractor's license.
- **A utility bill or lease** in the business name for any address you list.
- **Business tax or registration documents** that show the business name.

The point is to connect the name on your profile to a real, registered business that you operate.

### A live website that matches

Before you try again, make sure your website shows:

- the exact same business name as your profile,
- the same phone number,
- the same hours,
- the same services,
- and the same service areas.

If there is a mismatch, fix the website or fix the profile so they agree. We go deep on this in our post on matching your website to your Google Business Profile, coming next week.

### A phone number you actually answer

Use a phone number that belongs to the business and is answered during your listed hours. If it is listed on your website, your truck and your profile, all the better.

## How to Record a Better Verification Video

Google's exact instructions can change, and the verification screen will tell you what it wants for your profile. Read those instructions every time.

In general, a good verification video for a service-area business does three things in **one continuous recording**:

1. **Shows where you are.** Street signs, nearby landmarks or the outside of your shop or office, if you have one.
2. **Shows the business.** Your branded vehicle, equipment, tools and any signage. Open the truck. Show the gear you use on jobs.
3. **Shows that you manage it.** Unlocking the vehicle or the shop, accessing an area only staff can get into, or showing business documents with your name on them.

A few practical tips:

- **Plan the route before you hit record.** Walk through it once without recording.
- **Record in daylight** with steady hands. Go slowly.
- **Don't edit or cut the video.** One continuous take.
- **Keep it honest.** Do not borrow someone else's truck or shop. Show what is actually yours.

If your business is new and you don't have much branding yet, take the time to get some in place before you record. Trying to verify with a plain truck and nothing else to show is a tough position.

## How to Reapply the Right Way

If your profile was denied or suspended, here is the order we follow.

### Step 1: Read what Google sent you

Look at the email and the message in your profile dashboard. Sometimes Google names the issue. Sometimes it is general. Either way, start there.

### Step 2: Find the real problem

Go through the list above honestly:

- Is the name exactly your real business name, with no extra keywords?
- Is the address hidden if customers don't come to you?
- Is there a P.O. box or mailbox service involved?
- Is there a duplicate profile somewhere?
- Does your website match the profile?
- Did the video clearly show a real business you manage?

Usually one or two of these jump out.

### Step 3: Fix it before you ask for review

Change the name back to your real name. Hide the address. Update the website. Get branding on the truck. Whatever the problem is, fix it first.

Asking Google to review the same problem again rarely helps.

### Step 4: Request a review or try verification again

Google provides ways to request another look, including an appeals process for suspended profiles, through Business Profile help. Submit your request with clear, honest information and the documents Google asks for.

### Step 5: Leave it alone while Google reviews

Avoid making more edits while the review is in progress. Then wait. Reviews can take time.

### What not to do

- **Don't create a brand new profile** to get around a denial. Duplicate profiles create more problems.
- **Don't invent an address** or use a virtual office to look like a storefront.
- **Don't pay someone who promises guaranteed verification** or offers to "unsuspend" profiles through back channels.

The clean, honest route is slower sometimes. It is also the only one that lasts.

## Once You Are Verified: Protect the Profile

Getting verified is the start, not the finish.

A few habits help keep the profile healthy:

- **Keep your name honest.** Don't add keywords later.
- **Keep hours current,** including holiday hours.
- **Add real photos** of your crew, trucks and jobs.
- **Ask every happy customer for a review,** and reply to the reviews you get.
- **Keep your website and profile in sync** whenever something changes.

Once the profile is verified, it becomes one of your most important ways to show up in local search. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a service-area business have a Google Business Profile without showing an address?

Yes. Google's guidelines say service-area businesses that don't serve customers at their address should hide it and list their service areas instead. You still provide an address for verification, but it isn't shown publicly.

### Why does Google keep asking me for video verification?

Google chooses which verification methods are available for each profile. For many businesses, video is the main option. If it keeps failing, the issue is usually what the video shows, so focus on clearly showing your location, your branded vehicle and equipment, and proof that you manage the business, all in one continuous recording.

### Do I need a website to verify my Google Business Profile?

Google doesn't strictly require one. But a live website with the same name, phone, hours, services and service areas as your profile makes your business easier to verify and easier for customers to trust.

### Can I put keywords like my city in my business name?

Not unless they are actually part of your real business name. Google's guidelines say the profile name should match the name you use in the real world. Adding keywords or locations that aren't part of your name is against the guidelines.

### How long does a review or appeal take?

It varies, and Google doesn't guarantee a timeline. Make your fixes first, submit once with clear information, and avoid editing the profile while it is under review.
