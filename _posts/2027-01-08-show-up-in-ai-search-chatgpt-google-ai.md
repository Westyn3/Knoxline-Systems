A homeowner's water heater starts leaking on a Sunday night.

A few years ago, they would have typed "water heater repair near me" into Google and scrolled through the results.

Today, some of them still do exactly that. Others open ChatGPT and type, "Who's a good plumber in Maryville, TN who can fix a leaking water heater?" Others see an AI-generated summary at the top of their Google results before they ever reach the regular listings.

Home-service owners have started asking us a fair question:

**"How do I make sure my business shows up in those AI answers?"**

Here's the honest answer up front: **nobody controls what AI tools say.** Not us, not any agency, not the business itself. Anyone promising guaranteed placement in ChatGPT or Google's AI Overviews is selling something they can't deliver.

But that doesn't mean you're helpless.

AI tools have to get their information from somewhere. The businesses that are easy for them to find, understand and trust are the ones with clear, consistent, accurate information across the web. That's the same foundation good local SEO has always required.

This post explains how AI search works in plain English and what a home-service business can actually do about it.

## What "AI Search" Means Right Now

"AI search" is a loose term that covers a few different things:

- **Google AI Overviews:** AI-written summaries that can appear at the top of some Google search results, often with links to the sources used.
- **Chat assistants** like ChatGPT, Google's Gemini, Microsoft Copilot and Perplexity, where someone types a question in plain language and gets a written answer.
- **AI features inside maps and other apps**, which change often.

These tools work differently from each other, and they change frequently. Some search the web in real time to answer a question. Some rely more on what they learned during training. Many do a mix.

We're not going to pretend to know the inner workings of every system. What matters for a local business is the common thread:

**AI tools build answers from information that already exists online.**

If information about your business is clear, consistent and available, you have a better chance of being part of that answer. If it's vague, missing or contradictory, you make it harder.

## Why This Is Mostly Good News

A lot of articles about AI search sound alarming. "SEO is dead." "Websites don't matter anymore."

We see it differently.

The things that help a business show up in AI answers are almost the same things that help it show up in regular Google results and the map pack:

- A clear website that explains what you do and where
- An accurate, complete Google Business Profile
- Real reviews from real customers
- Consistent business information across the web
- Structured data that spells out your business details
- Pages that answer the questions customers actually ask

If you've been doing local SEO the right way, you're not starting over. You're building on the same foundation.

If you haven't, the work you do now helps in both places.

## 1. Make Your Business Information Consistent Everywhere

This is the most important point in the whole post.

When an AI tool (or Google) tries to figure out who you are, it may look at many sources: your website, your Google Business Profile, other map listings, directories, review sites, social profiles and mentions on other websites.

If all of those agree on your name, phone, service area and what you do, the picture is clear.

If your website says "Smith Roofing LLC," your Google profile says "Smith Roofing & Gutters," an old directory lists a phone number you stopped using in 2019 and your Facebook page says you serve a county you dropped two years ago, the picture gets muddy.

**Consistency tells every system that you are exactly who you say you are.**

### What to Check

- **Name:** the same real business name everywhere. No keywords stuffed in.
- **Phone:** one main number, the same everywhere.
- **Address or service area:** accurate and matching.
- **Hours:** current on every listing.
- **Website:** the same URL everywhere.
- **Services:** the same core services described in a similar way.

We went through this in detail, using our own website as the example, in [why your website should match your Google Business Profile](/blog/match-website-to-google-business-profile/). When we rebuilt our site, we matched the legal name, phone, hours, all 20 service areas and our categories to the profile exactly.

### Don't Forget Other Map Platforms

Google isn't the only source of business listings. Bing and Apple Maps have their own business listing tools. Since different AI tools may draw on different sources, it's worth making sure your business is claimed and accurate on the major platforms, not just Google.

## 2. Keep Your Google Business Profile Complete and Accurate

For Google's own AI features, your Google Business Profile is likely to be one of the most important sources of information about your business.

Make sure it includes:

- Your real business name
- The right primary category and accurate additional categories
- Your services, described in plain language
- Your service areas (service-area businesses can hide their street address)
- Accurate hours, including holiday hours
- A clear description
- Real photos of your work

An incomplete profile leaves gaps. Gaps are where the wrong information, or no information, ends up in an answer.

## 3. Add Structured Data to Your Website

Structured data, also called schema markup, is code that describes your business in a standardized format machines can read directly.

Instead of hoping a system correctly interprets "We're open weekdays from 7 to 6," structured data states it plainly: these are the opening hours. Instead of guessing your service area from a paragraph, it lists the areas you serve.

For a local service business, useful structured data often includes:

- **A business type** (LocalBusiness or a more specific type like ProfessionalService) with your name, phone, hours, logo and service areas
- **sameAs** links that connect your website to your Google Business Profile and other official profiles
- **FAQ markup** on pages with real questions and answers
- **Breadcrumbs** that show how your pages are organized
- **Article markup** on blog posts

On our own site, we added business schema (ProfessionalService) with our legal name, hours, service areas, logo, founder, founding date and sameAs links including a permanent Google Maps link. We also added WebSite schema, breadcrumbs, Article schema on posts and FAQ schema on service pages. Google's Rich Results Test and the Schema.org validator both returned zero errors.

Structured data doesn't guarantee anything. Google says it uses structured data to understand page content, and it's a clean, unambiguous way to state the facts about your business. We explained it in plain English in [schema markup for local service businesses](/blog/schema-markup-for-local-service-businesses/).

## 4. Build Clear Service and Area Pages

When a homeowner asks an AI tool, "Who does septic inspections in Lenoir City?", the tool needs to find information connecting a business to that service and that place.

If your website has one homepage that says "We do it all!" and a list of 15 services in a single paragraph, that connection is weak.

If your website has a dedicated septic inspections page that explains what the inspection includes, when homeowners need one, and which areas you serve, the connection is much clearer.

### Service Pages

Each main service deserves its own page with:

- A clear heading naming the service
- A plain explanation of what it is and when someone needs it
- What the process looks like
- The areas where you offer it
- Common questions and honest answers
- An obvious way to call or request service

### Area Pages

If you do substantial work in specific towns, pages about your work in those areas can help, as long as they contain genuinely useful local information. Pages that are copies of each other with only the town name changed don't help customers and aren't worth building.

## 5. Answer Real Questions in Plain Language

People talk to AI tools the way they talk to a person. They ask full questions:

- "How often should I get my septic tank pumped?"
- "Is it worth repairing a 15-year-old AC unit?"
- "What should I do if my roof is leaking during a storm?"
- "How long does a water heater replacement take?"

Your customers already ask you these questions on the phone and in the driveway. Write them down. Then answer them on your website, in FAQ sections on service pages or in blog posts.

A few tips:

- **Answer directly.** Put the answer near the top, then explain.
- **Be honest.** If the answer is "it depends," explain what it depends on.
- **Use your own experience.** You know the trade. Write like it.
- **Keep it readable.** Short paragraphs, clear headings.

Clear, direct answers are easy for a person to read and easy for a system to understand and summarize.

## 6. Keep Real Reviews Coming

Reviews are one of the clearest signals that a business is real, active and trusted.

When someone asks an AI tool for a "good" or "reliable" plumber, the tool may draw on review information to shape its answer. Even when it doesn't, a customer who hears your name from an AI tool will often look you up on Google next. Your reviews are what they'll see.

A steady flow of real reviews, with a reply to each one, helps in every case.

Our client ALLCON Septic & Grading, a Georgia septic and grading company, went from no website and virtually no Google presence to 247 Google reviews at 4.9 stars. That took a consistent process over time, not a shortcut.

If you need a process, we laid one out in [a Google review system that runs itself](/blog/google-review-system-for-home-service-businesses/). And remember: buying reviews, offering incentives for reviews, or only asking happy customers all violate Google's policies.

## 7. Make Sure Your Site Can Actually Be Read

This sounds basic, but it trips up more websites than you'd expect.

Some websites put important information inside images, behind scripts that load slowly, or in formats that are hard for automated systems to read. If your phone number, services and service area only appear in a picture of a flyer, a person can read it but a machine may not.

Good practices:

- Put key information in real text on the page.
- Make sure pages load quickly and work on mobile.
- Use clear headings that describe what each section is about.
- Keep your site accessible to search engines (check Google Search Console for indexing problems).

This is one reason we hand-build sites in plain HTML and CSS. The content, headings, metadata and structured data are all written directly into the page, with nothing in the way.

## What You Can't Control

It's worth being clear about the limits.

- **You can't choose what an AI tool says.** Answers can vary from one question to the next, even for the same person.
- **You can't pay for placement** in organic AI answers the way you pay for ads.
- **Answers can be wrong.** AI tools sometimes get business details wrong. Keeping your information consistent everywhere is your best defense.
- **The tools change constantly.** What's true about one tool this month may be different in six months.

So we'd encourage you not to chase AI search as a separate project. Treat it as one more reason to get your foundation right.

## How to Check What AI Tools Say About You

You can do a simple check yourself:

1. Open a few AI tools: ChatGPT, Google (look for AI Overviews), Gemini, Copilot or Perplexity.
2. Ask questions a customer would ask: "Who does roof repair in Oak Ridge, TN?" or "Recommend a septic company near Farragut."
3. Also ask directly about your business: "What do you know about [your company name]?"
4. Note whether you appear, and whether the information is accurate.

If the information is wrong, trace it back. Often the source is an old listing, an outdated website page or a profile that was never updated. Fix the source.

Don't read too much into one answer. Run the same checks every few months and look for patterns.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I pay to show up in ChatGPT or Google AI Overviews?

Not in the organic answers. Some platforms offer advertising, and that may expand over time, but the organic answer itself isn't something a business can buy. The practical path is clear, consistent information across the web.

### Is SEO still worth it if people use AI tools?

Yes. Many people still use regular Google search and the map pack. And the things that help in AI search, like a clear website, an accurate Google Business Profile, reviews and structured data, are the same foundation that local SEO is built on.

### Does schema markup help with AI search?

Structured data states your business facts in a standard format that machines can read directly. It doesn't guarantee you'll appear in an AI answer, but it removes ambiguity about who you are, where you work and when you're open.

### What if an AI tool says something wrong about my business?

Find where the wrong information came from and fix it there: an old directory listing, a social profile, an outdated website page. Keeping every listing consistent is the best way to reduce errors over time.
