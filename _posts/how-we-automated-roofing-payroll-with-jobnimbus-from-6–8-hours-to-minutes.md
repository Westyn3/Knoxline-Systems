Payroll shouldn't take an entire evening when the information needed to calculate it already exists inside your CRM.

But that's exactly the situation one of our clients was facing.

Greater Roofing Solutions, a roofing company operating across two locations in Alabama, was using JobNimbus to manage its jobs and operational data. The information needed for payroll was already there—but turning that information into an accurate payroll report was still largely a manual process.

Every pay period meant cross-referencing jobs, commissions, commission splits, bonuses, and weekly guarantees.

The result?

\*\*Roughly 6–8 hours of payroll work every pay period.\*\*

Knoxline Systems built an automated payroll system around the JobNimbus data they were already entering.

Instead of spending hours building payroll, the owner can now review a finished payroll sheet, verify the numbers, and approve it.

Here's how it works—and why the same approach can be useful for other roofing and home-service businesses using JobNimbus.

## The Problem: The Data Was Already in JobNimbus

This is something we see frequently when looking at a company's workflow.

The business doesn't necessarily have a data problem.

It has a \*\*data movement problem\*\*.

Greater Roofing Solutions already had the information required to calculate payroll.

Their team was already entering job information into JobNimbus as part of their normal workflow.

The challenge was everything that happened afterward.

To prepare payroll, information had to be pulled together and reconciled across multiple pieces of the business.

That included things such as:

- completed jobs,
- job values,
- salesperson commissions,
- commission splits,
- bonus tiers,
- location-specific information,
- and weekly guarantee calculations.

Individually, none of those calculations were impossible.

The problem was having to perform them repeatedly.

Every pay period.

Across two locations.

As the business grew, a process that could technically be handled with spreadsheets became a significant administrative burden.

## The Old Roofing Payroll Process

A manual payroll process often grows gradually.

It starts with a spreadsheet.

Then another calculation gets added.

Then a new commission structure.

Then someone needs a bonus calculated differently.

Then the company opens another location.

Eventually, payroll isn't just payroll anymore.

It's a multi-step data reconciliation process.

For this roofing company, preparing payroll meant spending hours cross-referencing information that ultimately originated from the company's JobNimbus data.

That creates two problems.

The obvious problem is \*\*time\*\*.

Six to eight hours every pay period adds up quickly.

But the second problem is just as important:

\*\*Every manual step creates another opportunity for an error.\*\*

If information is copied incorrectly, a formula gets changed, a job is missed, or an outdated export is used, payroll can be affected.

And payroll is one of the last places a business owner wants uncertainty.

## The Better Question: Why Are We Typing This Again?

When we look at a repetitive business process, one of the first questions we ask is:

\*\*Where does this information originally come from?\*\*

If an employee is typing a number into a payroll spreadsheet that already exists inside JobNimbus, there may be no reason for someone to type it again.

The CRM should be able to provide that information.

That's the foundation of the payroll automation we built.

Instead of treating JobNimbus and payroll as two separate processes, we connected them.

JobNimbus became the source of the operational data.

The payroll system became the destination.

The automation handles what used to happen manually in between.

## How the JobNimbus Payroll Automation Works

The workflow starts with the information the roofing team is already entering into JobNimbus.

That is important.

We didn't want to create another system employees had to maintain.

We wanted the existing workflow to produce more value.

The payroll process pulls the necessary job information and applies the business rules required to calculate payroll.

Those rules include commission calculations, commission splits, bonus tiers, and weekly guarantee reconciliation.

The end result is a prepared payroll sheet.

Instead of:

\*\*export → copy → paste → calculate → reconcile → check\*\*

the process becomes:

\*\*JobNimbus → automated calculations → finished payroll sheet → review → approve\*\*

The business owner is still in control of payroll.

Automation simply removes the hours of repetitive work required to prepare it.

## Automating Roofing Sales Commissions

Commission structures are one of the reasons payroll can become complicated for roofing companies.

A salesperson's pay may depend on more than a single percentage.

There may be:

- different commission rates,
- split commissions,
- performance bonuses,
- thresholds,
- job-specific rules,
- or other compensation structures.

Those rules are manageable when there are only a handful of jobs.

They become much more time-consuming when the company is processing a larger volume across multiple employees and locations.

The important thing about automation is that these rules can be defined once.

Then the system applies them consistently.

If a job qualifies for a particular commission structure, the appropriate calculation can be applied.

If the commission is split, that can be accounted for.

If a bonus tier is reached, that logic can be included.

Instead of manually remembering and recalculating the compensation rules every pay period, the system handles the repeatable logic.

## Automatically Reconciling the Weekly Guarantee

Greater Roofing Solutions also had a weekly guarantee that needed to be incorporated into payroll.

This is another good example of why business automation is rarely just about "connecting two apps."

The connection itself isn't the valuable part.

The \*\*business logic between the systems\*\* is.

A generic software integration might be able to move a job record from one place to another.

But a useful operational system needs to understand what that job means for this particular company's payroll process.

That includes reconciling the weekly guarantee against the appropriate compensation calculations.

Once those rules are defined, the system can perform the same reconciliation every pay period without requiring the owner to rebuild it manually.

## Two Locations Without Two Separate Payroll Headaches

Operating multiple locations makes manual reporting more difficult.

The company needs visibility across the entire organization while still being able to understand what is happening at each location.

Without a connected system, that often means more spreadsheets, more filters, more exports, and more reconciliation.

The payroll workflow we built accounts for the company's multi-location structure as part of the process.

That allows the business to maintain the operational detail it needs without turning payroll into two completely separate manual processes.

This is one of the biggest benefits of building automation around the company's actual workflow instead of forcing the business into a generic template.

## How Much Time Is Manual Payroll Actually Costing?

Six hours every pay period doesn't necessarily sound catastrophic.

But consider what happens over an entire year.

If payroll is processed every two weeks, that's 26 pay periods.

At six hours per payroll cycle, that's:

\*\*156 hours per year.\*\*

At eight hours:

\*\*208 hours per year.\*\*

That's roughly \*\*four to five full 40-hour workweeks\*\* spent preparing payroll.

And that's before considering the interruptions, corrections, reconciliation, and mental load associated with the process.

Automation doesn't need to eliminate an entire employee position to create meaningful value.

Sometimes giving an owner or manager hundreds of hours back every year is more than enough justification.
