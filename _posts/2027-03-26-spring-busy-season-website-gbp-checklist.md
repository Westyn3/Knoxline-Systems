Every home-service owner knows the feeling.

One week the schedule has gaps. A few weeks later, the phone won't stop ringing.

Spring does that.

Roofs that made it through winter start leaking. The first warm days send homeowners to the thermostat, and some of them find out their AC isn't working. Spring rain soaks yards and shows which septic systems and drain fields are struggling. Lawns and landscaping come back to life, and so do the calls for help.

The demand is coming either way.

The question is whether your website and Google Business Profile are ready to catch it.

Here's the problem with waiting: once the busy season starts, nobody has time to fix the website. You're running crews, answering calls and juggling estimates. Whatever condition your online presence is in when the rush hits is roughly the condition it stays in until summer.

**Spring is won in March, not May.**

This checklist walks through what to check now, while there's still time to do it right.

## Why Spring Matters So Much for Home-Service Search

Homeowner searches tend to follow the season and the weather.

- **Roofing:** spring storms and winter damage that finally shows up as a leak or missing shingles.
- **HVAC:** the first warm days, when homeowners switch to cooling and discover problems.
- **Septic:** wet ground puts pressure on systems, and homeowners notice slow drains, odors or soggy areas over the drain field.
- **Grading and drainage:** spring rain shows where water is going where it shouldn't.
- **Landscaping and lawn care:** the growing season starts and people want their yards back.
- **Plumbing:** outdoor spigots and irrigation get turned back on after winter.

Each of those turns into searches. "Roof leak repair." "AC not cooling." "Septic pumping near me." "Yard drainage contractor."

If your Google presence is ready, those searches can become calls. If it isn't, they become calls for someone else.

## Part 1: Your Google Business Profile

For many local searches, the first thing a homeowner sees is the map results. That's your Google Business Profile. Start here.

### Check your hours

This sounds basic. It matters.

- **Are your regular hours correct?** If you're adding Saturday hours or extending weekday hours for the season, update them.
- **Do your hours match your website exactly?** A profile that says you close at 5 while your site says 6 creates confusion.
- **Add special hours** for any upcoming holidays or closures.

On our own site, we keep our website hours identical to our profile, Monday through Friday, 7 AM to 6 PM. Same with our name, phone and the 20 service areas listed on the profile. Consistency tells Google you are exactly who you say you are. We cover that in depth in [Why Your Website Should Match Your Google Business Profile 100%](/blog/match-website-to-google-business-profile/).

### Review your services

Go through the services listed on your profile.

- Are your spring services listed? Roof inspections, AC tune-ups, septic pumping, drainage work, whatever you actually offer.
- Are there services listed that you no longer offer? Remove them.
- Do the descriptions explain the work in plain language?

### Check your categories

Your primary category should describe your core business. Secondary categories can cover other real services. Only choose categories that accurately describe what you do.

### Review your service area

If you're a service-area business, check the areas listed on your profile.

- Do they reflect where you actually work this year?
- Are you planning to push into new areas this spring? A good approach is to start where you already show up and expand as you win.

### Add fresh photos

Photos help homeowners see that you're a real, active business doing real work.

- Recent job photos, before and after if possible.
- Your trucks and equipment with your branding.
- Your crew at work, with their permission.

Skip stock photos. They don't show your work.

### Post a spring update

Google Business Profile lets you publish posts to your profile. A spring post is a simple way to show you're active:

- A seasonal service you offer, like spring AC tune-ups or septic inspections.
- A reminder about what homeowners should check after winter.
- A link to a relevant page on your website.

Keep it short, accurate and useful. Don't stuff it with keywords.

### Reply to recent reviews

Before the rush, catch up on any reviews you haven't replied to. Thank happy customers. Respond calmly and professionally to unhappy ones.

Once the season starts, keep replying. A habit is easier to keep than to restart.

## Part 2: Your Website

### Update or create seasonal pages

Ask yourself: when a homeowner searches for your spring services, do you have a page that answers them?

Examples:

- **Roofer:** a page about spring roof inspections or storm damage repair.
- **HVAC:** a page about AC tune-ups and cooling repair.
- **Septic:** pages for septic pumping and inspections, with information about why spring is a good time to check a system.
- **Grading:** a page about drainage problems and solutions.
- **Landscaping:** pages for spring cleanup and the services you offer most.

If the page already exists, review it. Is the information current? Is the phone number easy to find? Does it link to your contact page?

If the page doesn't exist, build it now. New pages take time to be found and ranked. Organic SEO usually takes months, so earlier is better. A page built in April may not be working for you until well into the season.

### Check your homepage message

Does your homepage reflect what you're offering right now? If spring is your busiest season for certain services, make sure they're easy to find from the homepage.

### Run a speed check on your phone

Your customers will be searching on their phones, often while dealing with a problem. Pull up your website on your own phone, using cellular data, not Wi-Fi.

- Does it load quickly?
- Can you read the text without zooming?
- Is the phone number tappable?
- Does anything slide sideways or get cut off?

Then run your site through Google PageSpeed Insights. When we checked our own site, the mobile performance score was 76 before speed fixes. The biggest problem was about 2.1 seconds of render-blocking requests, mostly from Google Fonts. We fixed it by self-hosting fonts with preload, loading analytics after the page and making the main headline paint immediately.

Your issues may be different. Large images, auto-playing videos and extra scripts are common culprits. We wrote about all of it in [Website Speed for Contractors](/blog/website-speed-for-contractors/).

### Test every contact path

This takes ten minutes and can save a season of lost leads.

- **Tap the phone number** on your website from a phone. Does it call the right number?
- **Submit every contact form.** Does the message actually arrive? Who gets it?
- **Check any quote request or booking forms.**
- **Check the links on your Google Business Profile.** Do they go to the right pages?

A broken form during busy season is invisible. Nobody calls to tell you your form doesn't work. They just call someone else.

### Check your metadata

For your most important spring pages, check:

- **Title tags** describe the service and area clearly.
- **Meta descriptions** explain what the page offers.
- **Structured data** is in place and passes Google's Rich Results Test.

On our own site, we added business schema, breadcrumbs, Article schema on posts and FAQ schema on service pages, and both the Rich Results Test and the Schema.org validator returned zero errors. Our Lighthouse SEO score was 100 on every page we tested. You don't need perfection to get calls, but clean metadata gives Google fewer reasons to be confused.

## Part 3: Reviews

Spring brings more jobs, and more jobs mean more chances to earn reviews.

Build the review ask into the job now, before the rush.

### Make it easy

- **Get your direct Google review link** from your Business Profile.
- **Put it on QR code cards** your crews can hand to customers at the end of a job.
- **Send it in a follow-up text or email** after the job is complete.

### Time the ask

The best time to ask is right after the work is done and the customer is happy. The roof is fixed. The AC is cold. The septic tank is pumped.

### Stay within Google's rules

- **Don't buy reviews.**
- **Don't offer incentives** for reviews.
- **Don't ask only happy customers** while steering unhappy ones away. That's review gating, and it's against Google's policies.
- **Don't trade reviews** with other businesses.

Just ask every customer, consistently.

ALLCON Septic & Grading, a Georgia septic and grading company we work with, grew to 247 Google reviews at 4.9 stars with a consistent review process as part of its foundation.

## Part 4: Call Tracking and Lead Tracking

Busy season is when you'll learn the most about where your leads come from, but only if you're tracking.

### Know your lead sources

At minimum, you want to know:

- How many calls came from Google Business Profile?
- How many calls and forms came from the website?
- How many came from ads, if you run them?
- Which services people called about?

### Be careful with phone numbers

If you use call tracking numbers, think through where they appear. Your business name, address if shown and phone number should be consistent between your website and Google Business Profile. If you add a tracking number to your profile, keep your main business number listed as well, and keep your website's main number consistent. When in doubt, keep it simple.

### Count real leads, not just clicks

This matters most if you run ads. When we audited the Google Ads account of a Georgia home-service company, nearly 40% of the reported "conversions" were map taps and direction clicks, not real leads. Make sure your reports count actual calls and form submissions.

## Part 5: Consider a Spring Ads Test

If you want more calls this spring than organic search can deliver yet, a focused Google Ads test can help.

Keep it controlled:

- **Pick one or two spring services** you want more of.
- **Target the areas you actually serve.**
- **Run ads during hours you can answer the phone.**
- **Send each ad to the matching page**, not your homepage.
- **Set up conversion tracking before spending money.**
- **Review results weekly** and adjust.

Ads are fast and flexible, but they stop when you stop paying. SEO builds slower but keeps working. We compared the two in last week's post, [Google Ads vs. SEO for Home-Service Companies](/blog/google-ads-vs-seo-home-service/).

## Part 6: Look at Search Console

If your website is connected to Google Search Console, spend twenty minutes there before the season starts.

- **Performance report:** which searches are you showing up for? Are spring searches starting to pick up?
- **Pages report:** are any important pages not indexed?
- **Sitemap:** is your sitemap submitted and working?

If you built new seasonal pages, use URL Inspection to request indexing.

When we watched our own Search Console after updating our site, impressions went from about 6 per day to about 29 per day in the first week, but clicks were still near zero at that point. Rankings move first. Clicks follow once pages reach page one. That's why getting spring pages up early matters.

## The Spring Checklist

Here's everything in one place.

### Google Business Profile

- Update hours, including any seasonal changes
- Confirm hours, name and phone match the website
- Review services and descriptions
- Check categories and service areas
- Add recent job photos
- Publish a spring post
- Reply to every recent review

### Website

- Update or create seasonal service pages
- Make spring services easy to find from the homepage
- Test speed on a phone and in PageSpeed Insights
- Tap-test every phone number
- Submit every form and confirm delivery
- Check titles, descriptions and structured data

### Reviews

- Get your direct review link
- Print QR code review cards
- Set up follow-up review requests
- Follow Google's review policies

### Tracking and Ads

- Confirm you can see calls and forms by source
- Keep phone numbers consistent
- Count real leads only
- Consider a small, focused ads test

### Search Console

- Check performance and indexing
- Submit or confirm your sitemap
- Request indexing for new pages

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When should I start preparing for spring busy season?

As early as you can. Late winter is ideal. New website pages take time to be found and ranked, and you'll have far less time to fix things once the busy season starts.

### Should I change my Google Business Profile hours for spring?

If your actual hours change, yes. Your profile should always reflect your real hours, and your website should match.

### Do spring posts on Google Business Profile help?

They keep your profile current and give homeowners useful information. Keep them accurate and relevant to the services you actually offer.

### Is it too late to start SEO for this spring?

It's never too late to start, but SEO takes months. Starting now helps you later this season and positions you better for next year. If you need calls right away, a focused ads test can help in the meantime.

### How many reviews should I aim for this spring?

There's no magic number. Focus on asking every customer consistently. Steady, recent reviews from real customers matter more than a burst followed by silence.
