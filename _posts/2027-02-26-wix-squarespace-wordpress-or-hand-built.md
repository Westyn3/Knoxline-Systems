Someone says, "You should just build it on Wix."

## What a Home-Service Website Actually Has to Do

Before comparing platforms, it helps to agree on the job.

A home-service website is not a brochure. It's a sales tool that works while you're on a roof, in a crawlspace or under a sink.

When a homeowner's septic system backs up on a Saturday, they need to find you, trust you and call you. Fast.

That means your site needs to:

- **Load quickly on a phone**, often on a weak signal.
- **Say clearly what you do**, with a page for each major service.
- **Say clearly where you work**, with service areas that match reality.
- **Match your Google Business Profile**: same name, phone, hours and service areas.
- **Give Google clean signals**: good titles, descriptions, headings and structured data.
- **Make calling or requesting a quote obvious**, with no hunting.

Keep that list in mind. Every platform below can technically produce a website. The difference is how easily it lets you do those six things well, and how much control you keep over the details.

## Wix

Wix is a drag-and-drop website builder. You pick a template, move blocks around, type in your text and publish.

### Where Wix works well

- **Getting something live quickly.** If you have nothing today, a basic Wix site is better than no site.
- **Owner editing.** You can change text and photos yourself without calling anyone.
- **Hosting is included.** You don't have to think about servers.
- **Built-in tools.** Forms, booking widgets and basic SEO settings are available inside the platform.

### Where Wix gets harder

- **Control over the code.** You work inside Wix's system. You can adjust many SEO settings, but you don't control the underlying HTML the way you do on a hand-built site.
- **Page weight.** Builder platforms tend to load a lot of scripts to make the drag-and-drop editor and widgets work. That can make it harder to get a fast mobile score.
- **Moving later.** Your design lives inside Wix. If you leave, you're rebuilding the site somewhere else, not exporting it.

### Who Wix fits

A brand-new business with a tight budget, an owner who wants to tinker, and a plan to upgrade once the company is established.

## Squarespace

Squarespace is also a hosted builder, known for polished templates and clean design.

### Where Squarespace works well

- **It looks good out of the box.** The templates are attractive and consistent.
- **Simple to manage.** Like Wix, hosting and security are handled for you.
- **Good for visual work.** Photo-heavy businesses, like remodelers showing finished kitchens, can look sharp quickly.

### Where Squarespace gets harder

- **Templates set the rules.** You can customize a lot, but you're working within the template's structure. Building a specific page layout for each service and each area can feel like fighting the tool.
- **Same code-control limits** as other hosted builders. You manage settings, not the HTML itself.
- **Speed takes effort.** Big, beautiful images and template scripts can slow a page down if nobody manages them.

### Who Squarespace fits

A business where design and photos carry most of the sale, with a small number of pages and an owner who wants to manage it themselves.

## Google Sites

Google Sites is Google's free, very simple site builder. A lot of owners assume that because it's made by Google, it must help with Google rankings.

We don't see it that way.

Google ranks pages based on what's on them and how useful they are, not on which company's tool built them.

### Where Google Sites works well

- **It's free and very easy.** You can have a simple page up in an afternoon.
- **Fine for internal use.** Employee handbooks, team resources and simple info pages are a good fit.

### Where Google Sites gets harder

- **Very limited SEO control.** Fine-grained control over titles, descriptions and structured data is limited compared to other options.
- **Limited design and structure.** Building out a full set of service pages and area pages is awkward.
- **Not built as a lead machine.** It's a simple publishing tool, not a marketing website platform.

### Who Google Sites fits

Honestly, very few home-service businesses that are serious about getting calls from search. It can work as a placeholder for a few weeks. We wouldn't build a growth plan on it.

## WordPress

WordPress powers a huge share of the web, and for good reason. It's flexible, there are thousands of themes and plugins, and almost any developer has worked with it.

### Where WordPress works well

- **Flexibility.** With the right developer, WordPress can do almost anything.
- **SEO plugins.** Popular plugins make it easier to set titles, descriptions and some structured data.
- **You can own it.** Self-hosted WordPress lets you move hosts and keep your content.
- **Big talent pool.** If one developer disappears, another can usually pick up the work.

### Where WordPress gets harder

- **Plugin pile-up.** Many WordPress sites end up running a page builder, a slider, a forms plugin, a security plugin, an SEO plugin and a caching plugin. Each one adds code and something else to update.
- **Maintenance is real.** WordPress core, themes and plugins need regular updates. Skip them and you risk broken pages or security problems.
- **Theme bloat.** Many off-the-shelf themes are built to do everything for everybody. That usually means loading code your site doesn't need.
- **Quality varies widely.** A well-built WordPress site can be excellent. A rushed one built on a heavy theme can be slow and messy.

### Who WordPress fits

A business that wants a lot of features, has a developer or agency maintaining it every month, and understands that "flexible" also means "more moving parts."

## Hand-Built HTML and CSS

This is what we do at Knoxline Systems. A hand-built site is written directly in HTML and CSS, page by page, without a drag-and-drop builder or a heavy theme underneath.

### Where hand-built works well

- **Full control over SEO and metadata.** Every title tag, meta description, heading, image attribute and piece of structured data is exactly what we put there. Nothing is generated by a plugin we don't control.
- **Speed.** There's no builder framework to load. The page carries only the code it needs. That gives a strong starting point for mobile performance.
- **Structured data done right.** We can add business schema, breadcrumbs, FAQ schema and Article schema by hand, and test every page.
- **Fewer moving parts.** No plugin updates. No theme conflicts. Fewer things that break on their own.
- **Portable.** Plain HTML and CSS files can be hosted almost anywhere.

### Where hand-built gets harder

We'd be doing you a disservice if we pretended there were no tradeoffs.

- **You usually don't edit it yourself in a visual editor.** Changes go through your developer. For most busy owners, that's a feature, not a bug, but it matters if you love tinkering.
- **Quality depends entirely on who builds it.** Hand-coded doesn't automatically mean good. A sloppy hand-built site is still sloppy.
- **It needs a partner.** You want someone who will keep it current, not someone who builds it and vanishes.

### Who hand-built fits

A home-service business that depends on Google for calls, wants every SEO detail handled, and would rather spend time running jobs than maintaining a website.

## Platform Isn't Everything

Here's something a lot of web companies won't say: the platform matters less than what you do with it.

A well-managed WordPress site with clear service pages and a matching Google Business Profile will beat a neglected hand-built site.

A thoughtful Squarespace site will beat a WordPress site stuffed with plugins nobody has updated in two years.

What really moves the needle for a home-service business:

1. **Clear service pages.** One page per major service, written for the homeowner who needs it.
2. **Honest service area content.** Pages that help someone in a specific town know you come there, without copy-paste city pages.
3. **A complete, accurate Google Business Profile** that matches the website.
4. **A steady flow of legitimate reviews.**
5. **Speed and mobile usability.**
6. **Consistent updates**, like a regular blog, so the site doesn't go stale.

The platform either makes those things easier or makes them harder. That's the real comparison.

## How to Choose: A Simple Decision Guide

If you're still not sure, start here.

### Pick Wix or Squarespace if:

- You're just starting and need something live this month.
- You want to edit everything yourself.
- You're okay rebuilding later as the business grows.

### Pick Google Sites if:

- You need a temporary placeholder while a real site is built.
- That's about it for customer-facing use.

### Pick WordPress if:

- You need complex features like a customer portal or a large catalog.
- You have someone maintaining updates every month.
- You understand plugins add both features and risk.

### Pick hand-built if:

- Google search and Google Maps are a major source of your calls, or you want them to be.
- You want full control over titles, metadata, schema and speed.
- You'd rather have a partner handle the site than manage it yourself.

## Questions to Ask Any Web Designer

Whatever platform you lean toward, ask these before you sign:

- **Who owns the site and the domain?** You should.
- **Can I get my content if I leave?** On some platforms the answer is "not really."
- **How will my site match my Google Business Profile?** Name, phone, hours, services, service areas.
- **What structured data will you add, and how will you test it?**
- **How fast does it load on a phone, and how will you check?**
- **Who updates it after launch, and how much does that cost?**
- **Will you promise me rankings?** If the answer is yes, be careful. Nobody can honestly guarantee a Google position.

## If You're Already on a Builder

Many owners reading this are already on Wix or Squarespace. That's fine. You don't need to panic.

Start by checking a few things:

- Does every major service have its own page?
- Do your name, phone and hours match your Google Business Profile exactly?
- How does the site load on your own phone, on cellular data, not Wi-Fi?
- Is the phone number tappable at the top of every page?

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a hand-built website better for SEO than WordPress?

Not automatically. A hand-built site gives you full control over every SEO detail and starts lighter, which makes good results easier to reach. A carefully built and maintained WordPress site can also perform well. The quality of the work matters more than the label.

### Does Google favor sites built on Google Sites?

We're not aware of any reason it would. Google ranks pages on their content and usefulness. Google Sites is simply a tool, and a fairly limited one for marketing.

### Can I start on Wix and switch later?

Yes. Plenty of businesses do. Just plan the switch carefully, keep your content, and redirect old URLs to the new ones so you don't lose ground.

### How long before a new site shows up well in Google?

Organic SEO usually takes months. Around six months is a fair expectation to see a real picture. On our own site, rankings started moving within weeks, but clicks lag behind until pages reach the first page of results.

### Will I be able to make changes to a hand-built site?

Changes go through us. Most owners we work with prefer it that way: they send a note, and the update gets done correctly without breaking anything.
